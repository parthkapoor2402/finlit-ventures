## What commentators say the decision should depend on

These are expert opinions, not incidence rates.

- Economic Times, "Should you stop SIPs during market corrections?": volatility is generally a poor reason to stop; loss of income, a nearer goal, or a fund that no longer fits can be a reason; reducing is often framed as safer than a full stop because restarting is harder. https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/mf/analysis/should-you-stop-sips-during-market-corrections-heres-what-investors-need-to-know/articleshow/127525519.cms
- Mint, 2026, quoting Varun Gupta, CEO of Groww Mutual Fund: temporary income loss, medical emergency, or a cash-flow shock can justify a pause; a correction is part of equity investing and a weak reason to stop. https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/when-should-you-pause-your-sip-experts-explain-when-it-makes-sense-and-when-it-doesnt-11785041953658.html

## How to read this market

The relevant market is not 144 crore people and not 38.5 crore "financially aware" adults. It is people who already run a SIP and are about to change it. The observable outer bound is on the order of tens of lakhs of SIP discontinuations or completions per month, of which an unknown fraction are voluntary decisions. Unique investors are fewer than SIP accounts. FinLit's reachable set is a small subset of people who will open a research site or a portfolio-review conversation. That subset is sized as a scenario in `07_opportunity_sizing.md`, not as a fact.
