# 11 — Problem validation and impact

## Evidence chain

| Step | Statement | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | A SIP investor opens skip, pause, or cancel | Fact for the apps whose help centers document it |
| Unmet need | They must judge cash versus fear, and the effect on the next instalments, largely alone | Inference from help articles plus forum questions |
| Root cause | The confirm step is an instruction, not a branched explanation (H2) | Hypothesis, medium confidence |
| Alternatives | Help articles, forums, advisers, doing nothing | Fact that they exist; unknown how often they are used in the moment |
| Intervention | Reason branch plus deterministic consequence card before confirm | Recommendation |
| Behaviour | User can restate the consequence and then choose continue, reduce, or pause | Not yet measured |
| Customer outcome | A choice matched to the reason, including a correct pause | Hypothesis |
| Business outcome | A useful FinLit session that can lead to a paid review the user chooses | Hypothesis |

## Answers

- **Who:** salaried investors with a live SIP and a goal that is not immediate. Illustrative persona Ananya in `06`.
- **Situation:** they are about to skip, reduce, or cancel, often after seeing a red return or after a cash squeeze.
- **Trigger:** the intent, not the index level. A falling market with no intent to change the SIP is not an interruption.
- **Job:** choose the next instalment with the consequence in view.
- **Today:** a short confirm, sometimes a reason list and an OTP. Then, if unsure, a forum.
- **Cost of today:** a fear-based stop during a long horizon, or a cash-based stop with no restart date and a risk of auto-cancel. Both costs are plausible. Neither is quantified.
- **Why unresolved:** see `10`. Incumbents optimize execution. The public statistic does not isolate the decision.
- **Why timing matters:** after the skip, the instalment is gone and the lesson is a help article. Before the skip, the choice is still open. The decision is partly reversible (a SIP can be restarted) but the restart often does not happen, according to adviser commentary and forum replies. That is not a measured hazard rate.
- **Smallest useful intervention:** one screen. Reason, missed rupees, "this does not redeem units," restart date if pausing, three buttons.
- **Why FinLit:** the public brand is understand-before-action, and the footer says it is not an AMFI distributor, so it is not paid trail to keep the SIP alive. It also cannot embed in the broker. The honest product lives on FinLit until a partner exists.
- **Why now:** SIP accounts are about 10 crore and monthly contributions about ₹32,000 crore (August 2026 AMFI note). The habit is mainstream, and the confirm step is still thin. This is not a claim that stops are accelerating. July 2026's stoppage ratio fell, on the Economic Times reading.
- **Why trust it:** show inputs, label assumptions, allow the pause, do not predict returns, do not take a commission on the fund.
- **How it becomes harmful:** shame copy, a buried pause button, a fake precision goal date, a wrong platform rule, or anything that reads as "SEBI-registered advice" when the firm is not registered.
- **What would invalidate it:** usability tests where the card adds no comprehension, or proof the apps already do this branch well.

## Urgency

| Lens | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Time sensitivity | High once the user is in the flow. The next debit has a cutoff of a few days |
| Reversibility | Medium. Units already bought stay. A skipped debit is not retroactively invested. A SIP can be recreated |
| Financial exposure | The next few instalments, not the whole corpus. Lower than a redemption |
| Avoidable cost | Only the fear-based interruption. The cash-based interruption should happen |
| Emotion | High in forum titles. Not a measured rate |
| Frequency | The button is frequent. The mistake rate is unknown |
| Cost of waiting to understand | The confirm is the last cheap moment |

## Impact

Customer, if the card works: clearer reason, clearer rupee consequence, a restart plan when they pause, less confusion between skip and redeem. Not promised: higher returns.

Business, if anyone uses it: a concrete demonstration of FinLit's research loop, a content entry point, and a paid review only when the user asks. Retention of FinLit, not retention of the SIP, is the business metric. A well-informed pause is a successful session.

## Explicit non-goals for success

Lower redemption, fewer pauses, and higher SIP amounts are not success by themselves.
