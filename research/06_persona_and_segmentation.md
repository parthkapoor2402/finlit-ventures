# 06 — Persona and segmentation

Candidate segments were narrowed with the evidence in `05` and `08`. No interviews were conducted. The persona is an illustrative composite, not a quoted customer.

## Narrowing

| Stage | Who | Why kept or cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad market | Households aware of securities products (213 million in the SEBI 2025 survey) | Most are not investors. Out of scope for a SIP decision |
| Relevant market | Households that hold mutual funds or ETFs, about 6.7% nationally and about 18% in the top nine metros | They can face a SIP decision. Many hold a single product and are not "portfolio exit" customers |
| Behavioural segment | People with at least one active SIP who hit a skip, reduce, or cancel control | This is the moment. Account counts are a ceiling, not a headcount |
| Primary target | Salaried urban investor, goal more than about five years out, SIP large enough to feel and small enough that they have no adviser | SEBI: urban participation is higher; knowledge is limited; capital preservation is the default attitude. Expert sources: this is who pauses for the wrong reason and who also has real cash shocks |
| High-intent persona | Ananya, below | She is at the button, not browsing a blog |
| Secondary | The same person with a real one- to three-month cash gap | Must be designed for, or the product becomes a retention trick |

Cut from the initial target:

- First-time non-investors. Their job is starting, which is FinLit's 0→1 service, not this moment.
- Near-term liquidity cases as the *primary* design target. They need an easy pause and a restart date. They are a branch, not the campaign.
- Experienced multi-platform investors. They already compare Coin, Kuvera, and Groww. They are a poor first comprehension test.
- HNI discretionary clients. Wrong regulation, wrong frequency, and too close to the wording FinLit should not expand.

## Why this persona, not "millennials" or "Tier 2"

Age and city do not create the problem. The problem appears when three conditions coincide: an active SIP, a goal that is still distant, and a reason the current confirm screen does not force the user to name. Urban salaried investors are the practical first channel because that is where mutual-fund participation is higher and where FinLit can reach people with articles and a ₹99 review, not because Tier 2 is unimportant. The site's own Tier 2 story is an unsupported growth slogan for this decision.

## Persona — illustrative

**Ananya Shah, 31, Pune. Salaried. Not a real person.**

| Attribute | Illustrative detail | What it is anchored to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | Two equity SIPs, together ₹12,000 a month, started 18 months ago. House goal in about 8 years. Emergency fund of about two months' expenses | A typical direct-plan SIP size is not asserted as a statistic. The situation is a teaching case |
| Job to be done | Decide whether next month's instalments should run, shrink, or stop, without guessing | The moment in H2 |
| Experience | Uses one app. Knows "SIP" and "NAV." Cannot explain rupee-cost averaging without prompting | Consistent with 36% moderate-or-high knowledge among investors; not a personal measurement |
| Trigger | The app shows the fund down over the past month. A colleague says to "wait for the bottom" | Public thread pattern, not her quote |
| Current workflow | Opens the SIP, taps Skip or Cancel, sees a confirm and maybe a reason list, asks Reddit or the family WhatsApp if unsure | Help-center flows; forum threads |
| Functional need | What rupees will not be invested, what happens to the SIP if she skips again, and whether the goal date is even affected by one month | Arithmetic, not a forecast |
| Emotional need | Permission to pause if money is tight, and a way not to feel foolish if she continues | Mint's split between cash need and fear |
| Desired outcome | A choice she can explain to herself a week later | Informed completion, not a higher SIP |
| Constraints | Ten minutes, a phone, distrust of anyone who earns if she stays invested | FinLit is not an AMFI distributor, which helps this constraint |
| Fears | "I am buying a falling fund." "If I pause I will never restart." "This screen is trying to keep my money" | Forum language and the conflict-of-interest risk |
| Alternatives | Do nothing and let the SIP run; skip; cancel; ask a forum; book a human review | All exist today |
| Trust requirements | Show the inputs. Show what is not known. Let her pause anyway | Explainability requirement from the brief and from SEBI's advice boundary |
| Comprehension | Short sentences. No CAGR lecture on the first screen | H3 |
| Decision criteria | Horizon still long, salary intact, missed amount understandable, restart dated if she pauses | The card's content |
| Rejection | A lecture, a predicted return, a disabled pause button, or a request to link her broker before she sees anything | Dark-pattern and integration constraints |

## Secondary branch — same person, different month

Salary is delayed. The correct product behaviour is to make Pause the obvious allowed choice, ask for a restart month, and state the platform rule on consecutive skips if her app has one. Success is not continuation.
