# Storyboard

The deck argues that Skip Check should be tested, and it stops before any wireframe.

1. Opening strategy story, on warm ivory. Skip Check by FinLit Ventures is personalised clarity before an SIP decision. Investors consider changing a SIP for different personal reasons. Existing tools may show amounts and transaction options, and that does not explain the implications of the person’s reason. Skip Check addresses the gap with reason-aware, personalised decision support. The reason changes the emphasis, not the formula: current minus proposed, times one to twenty-four months, in whole rupees. The job is reconstructed from desk research, not an interview. The emphasis line is “Different reasons. One need: clarity before commitment.” Reasons named are product labels: expenses or priorities, market movements, plans or timeline, and wanting to understand the investment better. The path is decision support, not transaction execution. The investor compares, decides, and returns to their own app. Skip Check does not execute, pause, cancel, or restart a SIP. The business line is a hypothesis: the check is free and may create a pathway to published listing plans from ₹99 to ₹2,499. No conversion lift has been measured, and this prototype does not take payment. Supporting figures are August 2026 contributions of ₹32,297 crore and 10.02 crore contributing accounts, from the AMFI monthly note, and about 50.30 lakh discontinued or tenure-completed SIPs in July 2026, from Economic Times citing AMFI. That July count is not voluntary pauses from market worry or cash distress. Actors are the investor, the investing app, Skip Check, and FinLit Ventures. FinLit states it is not registered with SEBI or AMFI, and a compliance review of this prototype has not been completed. No founder name, and no interviews.

2. User research. Secondary sources and the live flow only. Public threads show a cash need and a possible correction, not how common either is. Help articles show that a stop can be mistaken for a withdrawal. July and August 2026 contributions are on one scale from zero. The journey is a candidate, not a watched session. The mapping to Keep, Reduce, and Pause is not a usability result. The closing line is an inference, not an interview finding.

3. One person, two months. Ananya Shah is a teaching composite from the research notes. The market-worry month and the cash-gap month are the same person. Pause is a legitimate result in the second month. The four outcomes are intended, and none is measured. SEBI’s survey is linked as background, not as her result.

4. Journey and hypotheses. The live order is the assessment: two landing doors, an optional reason that changes emphasis and not the formula, amounts, equal Keep / Reduce / Pause, What if, optional questions, a local recap, and a local reminder. Pause is a proposed ₹0. The bot explains and does not decide. Analytics stay in memory and store no amounts. The chart shows the four desk means. H2 is selected because it still helps if most pauses are legitimate. H2 is falsified if a cash-need user feels pressed to continue or cannot restate the arithmetic. research/17 still says the prototype was not built. docs/product-decisions.md describes the earlier card. informedChoiceRate belongs to that earlier event list and has no observed rate.

5. Problem framing. The canvas is the true problem, who, how we know, and value that is only potential. Why look now is a hypothesis, because contributions are still rising. The 38.5 crore TAM and “NCFE 2026” are rejected. The 27% literacy figure is 2019 fieldwork. Customer value and business value are both unmeasured.

6. What to validate next. The order is counsel, then usability, then distribution. None has been run. What we believe is separated from what is unproven, including the RICE reach of 200. The prototype address is linked and is not usage evidence. The deck stops before wireframes.
