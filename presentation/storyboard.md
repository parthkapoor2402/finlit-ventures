# Storyboard

The deck argues that Skip Check should be tested, and it stops before any wireframe.

1. Market landscape, on a white field. The problem says a cash need and a market scare meet the same thin confirm, so the person cannot see the next instalments, and Skip Check is built so they can restate the rupee difference, Pause stays available, and they still decide in their own app. What Skip Check includes states a need and an impact for the reason (emphasis only), equal Keep / Reduce / Pause, What if, the rupee line against a loss with units staying invested, optional clarity questions, a local recap, and the bot with the not-connected panel. The job is the reconstructed sentence, labeled as not an interview. Revenue says the check is free because a person will not start a paid conversation before the choice is clear, and the impact it is built to create is more connects for FinLit Ventures. Paid work those connects can become is listed from ₹99 to ₹2,499. This prototype does not take payment, and that lift has not been measured. Market buzz says contributions are still rising, so this is not an anti-panic product; that July’s 50.30 lakh figure is the wrong count of voluntary pauses; that public writing mixes a scare and a cash need, so both are explained and Pause stays; and that help articles still separate a stop from a redemption, so the screen says units generally stay invested. The July 2026 chart keeps 61.44 beside 50.30.

2. User research. Secondary sources and the live flow only. Public threads show a cash need and a possible correction, not how common either is. Help articles show that a stop can be mistaken for a withdrawal. July and August 2026 contributions are on one scale from zero. The journey is a candidate, not a watched session. The mapping to Keep, Reduce, and Pause is not a usability result. The closing line is an inference, not an interview finding.

3. User personas and expected outcomes. The project has one illustrative person, Ananya Shah, not two interviewed users. The market-worry month and the salary-delay month are the same teaching case. Product outcomes are what the live page is built to do. Business outcomes, including any path from the free check to plans listed from ₹99 to ₹2,499, are a hypothesis. The informed-choice rate is a proposal for an earlier flow and is not a result on this page.

4. Problem framing canvas. The true problem is that different reasons can meet one thin confirm. Ananya Shah’s market-worry month and salary-delay month are the same teaching case. Evidence is public threads, Kuvera and Groww help, a desk judgement that is not a user rating, and the July discontinuation count labeled as not voluntary pauses. Customer value is the live explanation. Business value, from a free check toward plans listed from ₹99 to ₹2,499, is a hypothesis. Contributions rose, so the page is not framed as a redemption crisis.

5. Solution ideation. The notes compare three concepts: a broker interrupt, Skip Check, and a review after a pause. Weighted desk scores are 3.65, 4.05, and 2.55. Skip Check is the recommendation because FinLit can ship it and a pause stays allowed. RICE inside that build uses a reach of 200 as an assumption. The rupee line scores 320, the reason and three choices score 140, and the restatement check scores 120. Conversion to plans from ₹99 to ₹2,499 is unmeasured.

6. What to validate next. The order is counsel, then usability, then distribution. None has been run. What we believe is separated from what is unproven, including the RICE reach of 200. The prototype address is linked and is not usage evidence. The deck stops before wireframes.
