# Slide 9 sources

The diagram follows the page `App` renders: `AssessmentApp`. Explore my options starts a decision session. I’m just exploring opens a sample at ₹12,000, ₹6,000, and 6 months, and the page says that sample is not the user’s SIP. A sample is not a qualified decision session.

The reason step can be skipped. The reason does not change the formula. Amounts are whole rupees. Months run from 1 to 24. An increase is rejected. Above ₹10,00,000 the page asks the user to confirm the digits. The difference is (current − proposed) × months. The ₹12,000, ₹3,500, 6 month case equals ₹51,000 less in planned contributions. That figure is illustrative. A pause uses ₹0 and differs by ₹72,000 on the same example.

Typed amounts stay in the browser. Page events do not include those amounts. A recap is written to this browser only after the user agrees to save it. SIP Check Bot is optional. If the user opens it, the calculated context is posted to Skip Check’s own `/api/sip-check` endpoint. There is no API to Groww, Coin, Kuvera, ET Money, or any other investing app. Skip Check does not execute, pause, or restart a SIP.

FinLit states it is not registered with SEBI or AMFI. A written compliance review has not been completed. An optional review is listed at ₹99. This prototype does not take payment and does not book that review. The calculator stays free.

The go-to-market weeks, the proposed landing line “Compare my SIP options,” the channel list, and the numeric gates are proposals for a future pilot. They are not a media plan, and no partnership is signed. The 8–10 person study is formative. Qualified intent, valid comparison, and Informed Choice Rate are proposed definitions, not measured results.
