# Slide 1 change log

The opening slide was rewritten as a product-strategy story. Slides 2–6 were not rebuilt.

## Content

The first block is now “Skip Check by FinLit Ventures,” with the line “Personalised clarity before an SIP decision.” It says investors reconsider a SIP for different personal reasons, that an amount on an investing app does not explain that reason, and that Skip Check is reason-aware decision support. The reason changes emphasis, not the formula.

The job to be done is labeled as reconstructed from desk research, not an interview. The emphasis line is “Different reasons. One need: clarity before commitment.” The four reasons named are the product’s own labels: expenses or priorities, market movements, plans or timeline, and wanting to understand the investment better.

The right block states the boundary: Skip Check explains the contribution difference and does not execute, pause, cancel, or restart a SIP. The four steps end in the person’s own app. No broker integration is claimed.

The business block says Skip Check is free and is meant to create a pathway to published listing plans from ₹99 to ₹2,499. The possible effect on purchases is labeled a hypothesis. No conversion lift is stated. This prototype does not take payment. Plan names such as “entry-level” were not added, because the project records prices (First Session ₹99 through 1 Year ₹2,499) rather than those labels.

The market block uses August 2026 SIP contributions of ₹32,297 crore and 10.02 crore contributing accounts, and says accounts are not people. The July 2026 figure of about 50.30 lakh discontinued or tenure-completed SIPs is attributed to Economic Times citing AMFI, and the slide says that count is not a count of voluntary pauses.

The founder’s name was removed from this slide.

## Design

The ivory field and forest bar stay. The product introduction is the largest block. The job and the execution boundary sit under it. The free-feature hypothesis and the two market figures sit lower. Source links remain on the AMFI August note and the Economic Times article.
