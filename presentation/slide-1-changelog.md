# Slide 1 change log

The opening slide was rewritten as the strategy story. Slides 2–6 were not rewritten.

## Content

The largest block is “SKIP CHECK BY FINLIT VENTURES,” with the line “Personalised clarity before an SIP decision.” Investors consider changing a SIP for different personal reasons. Existing tools and investing apps may show amounts and transaction options, and that does not explain the implications in the context of the person’s reason. Skip Check addresses that gap with reason-aware, personalised decision support. The selected reason changes the emphasis, not the formula. The difference in planned contributions is the current amount minus the proposed amount, times one to twenty-four months, in whole rupees.

The job to be done is labeled as reconstructed from desk research, not an interview. The statement is the reconsideration job: understand how keeping, reducing, or pausing future contributions changes planned investment amounts. The emphasis line is “Different reasons. One need: clarity before commitment.” The reasons named are product labels from `src/domain/assessment-reasons.ts`: “My expenses or priorities have changed.” “I’m unsure about market movements.” “My plans or timeline have changed.” “I want to understand my investment better.” The sentence says the product records these among its reasons. It does not claim they are the only reasons.

The process is headed “DECISION SUPPORT, NOT TRANSACTION EXECUTION.” Investors change SIPs in their own apps. Skip Check explains the contribution difference and does not execute, pause, cancel, or restart a SIP. The steps are: investor reconsidering a SIP, Skip Check comparison, investor decides, investor returns to their own app. No Groww, Kuvera, or Coin integration is claimed.

The business block is headed “A FREE FEATURE. A POTENTIAL CONVERSION DRIVER.” Skip Check is free. By addressing the person’s reason it aims to build trust and create a pathway to FinLit’s existing paid listing plans, published from ₹99 to ₹2,499. That range is the published list in `research/01_company_dossier.md` and `research/14_business_model_and_gtm.md`. The effect on exploring or purchasing a plan is labeled a hypothesis. No conversion lift is stated. This prototype does not take payment. Labels such as “entry-level” and “higher-tier” were not added.

The supporting market block is headed “A LARGE, ACTIVE SIP ECOSYSTEM.” It shows August 2026 SIP contributions of ₹32,297 crore and 10.02 crore contributing accounts, and it says the accounts are not people. About 50.30 lakh SIPs were discontinued or tenure-completed in July 2026. The slide says that count is not a count of voluntary pauses from market worry or cash distress. The July figure is attributed to Economic Times citing AMFI, and the slide says it was not re-read from an AMFI workbook.

Actors are the investor, the investing app, Skip Check, and FinLit Ventures. The founder’s name is not on this slide. The trust note says FinLit states it is not registered with SEBI or AMFI, that a compliance review of this prototype has not been completed, that the difference is in planned contributions rather than an investment loss, and that existing units generally remain invested unless redeemed separately. The slide does not claim Skip Check prevents poor decisions.

## Design

The canvas stays 13.333 by 7.5 inches. The field is warm ivory `#F6F1E8`. A slim forest bar carries SKIP CHECK and the six section names, with Market landscape active. The product card is the widest block and the only 26 pt line. The job card is wider than the process card. The free-feature card is wider than the two market figures. Source lines are 11 pt. Body copy is 13 or 14 pt. Georgia is used for the product name, the tagline, the job statement, and the card headings. Calibri is used for body copy. Hyperlinks sit on the AMFI August note and the Economic Times article.

The optional five-step visit flow was left off so the hypothesis sentences could stay at 13 pt.
